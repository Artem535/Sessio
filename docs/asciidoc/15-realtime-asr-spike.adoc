== ADR: Real-Time Transcription Engine — Spike Results and Recommendation

*Status:* Proposed

*Date:* 2026-10-06

*Issue:* #116 (spike code: `spikes/asr-model-bench/`, branch `spike/116-asr-model-bench`)

*Recommendation:* transcribe Russian speech with *GigaAM v3 behind a voice
activity detector (Silero VAD)*, one pipeline per audio track, producing text
phrase by phrase about 0.65 s after a speaker stops talking. If the product
needs words to appear while a person is still speaking, add *T-One* as a
streaming draft layer. Do not use Nemotron 3.5 Streaming or Parakeet TDT v3 as
the primary Russian engine. Use Whisper turbo only as an optional
post-session or noisy-audio engine.

=== Context

The commercial direction is an assistant inside one-to-one video appointments
(see issue #73 and the calls roadmap). The assistant needs a running
transcript of both participants, produced on the specialist's machine: session
audio is special-category personal data, so cloud speech APIs are not the
default. The call UI (#80) already receives one audio track per participant
from LiveKit, so speaker separation needs no diarization model.

Open questions before any product work:

* Which open model gives the best Russian accuracy at an acceptable CPU and
  memory cost next to a running video call?
* Is a true streaming model needed, or is "voice activity detector plus an
  offline model" good enough?
* Can the winner be embedded in the C++/Qt application?

=== Candidates

All models run through one runtime, `sherpa-onnx` 1.13.8 (ONNX Runtime, CPU),
with 4 threads, so that the comparison is about the models and not the runtime.

=== Method

*Machine.* AMD Ryzen 7 7840HS (16 threads), 27 GB RAM, Linux, no discrete GPU.
Every run used 4 threads and ran alone on the machine.

*Test sets.*

* `fleurs-clean`: 200 random utterances (seed 0, at most 25 s) from the Russian
  test split of Google FLEURS, read speech, clean.
* `fleurs-babble10`: the same utterances mixed with speech from three other
  utterances ("babble") at 10 dB signal-to-noise ratio.
* `podlodka`: 63 utterances (at most 25 s) from `bond005/podlodka_speech`,
  spontaneous speech from an IT podcast with technical vocabulary and English
  loanwords. This is the closest available proxy for conversation.
* `podlodka-long`: the Podlodka clips joined into 10 recordings of 59–121 s
  with random 0.4–1.0 s pauses. This tests a VAD on continuous audio.

All audio is resampled to 16 kHz once at load time. The Podlodka dataset
declares no licence, so it is used locally and is not stored in the repository.

*Metrics.*

* *WER / CER*: word and character error rate, computed over the whole test
  set. Text is lowercased, `ё` is folded to `е`, punctuation is removed. No
  number normalisation is applied, so "2000" against "две тысячи" counts as an
  error for every model equally.
* *RTF*: decode time divided by audio duration; below 1 means faster than
  real time.
* *cores*: average number of CPU cores busy while decoding.
* *RSS MB*: growth of process memory caused by loading and running the model.
* *first text*: for streaming models, the moment the first non-empty partial
  result is available minus the speech onset. Audio is fed in 100 ms chunks.
  Compute that cannot keep up with the audio makes the latency grow, so the
  number is honest even for slow models. Not meaningful for VAD pipelines.
* *final lag*: time after the last audio sample until the final text exists.
* *phrase lag*: for VAD pipelines, time from the end of a phrase (when the
  speaker stopped) to its text. It includes the 0.4 s of silence the VAD waits
  for before closing a phrase.

=== Findings

*Accuracy.* GigaAM v3 is the most accurate on spontaneous Russian: 7.5% WER
on Podlodka against 9.8% for Parakeet and 14.0% for the best streaming model
(T-One). Whisper turbo is the most accurate on clean read speech (4.0%) and in
babble noise (7.2%), but loses to GigaAM on spontaneous speech (9.0% against
7.5%). Parakeet degrades most in noise among the offline models (14.1% against
9.1–9.4% for GigaAM). Streaming models are 6.5–10.6 points behind the best offline
model on spontaneous speech and degrade strongly in noise (T-One 33.0%,
Nemotron 23–27%).

*Cost.* GigaAM is the lightest model: RTF 0.03 and 220–420 MB of memory.
T-One is similarly cheap (RTF 0.04, 360–520 MB). Parakeet needs about 1.3 GB.
Nemotron needs about 950 MB and keeps 6–7 cores busy although it was
configured for 4 threads; the 160 ms variant needs RTF 0.37–0.39, close to
the limit for a machine that also runs a video call. Whisper needs 2.3–2.7 GB
and RTF 0.3, which means 3–5 s of delay per short phrase.

*A VAD costs little accuracy.* On pre-cut clips GigaAM RNN-T scores 7.5%; on
continuous recordings cut by the real VAD it scores 8.4%. Parakeet goes from
9.8% to 11.3%. On the same recordings the streaming models score 15.2–17.4%.
GigaAM therefore stays ahead of every streaming model when it runs behind a VAD.

*Latency of the VAD pipeline.* Text for a phrase is available 0.62–0.64 s
(median) and 0.84–0.87 s (95th percentile) after the speaker stops, of which
0.4 s is the VAD's own silence window. Streaming models show first text about
1 s after speech starts and keep updating it while the person talks.

*Nemotron chunk size.* 560 ms gave the best accuracy of the three variants and
a first-text delay of about 1.1 s. The 160 ms variant saves 0.3 s of delay and
costs three times the CPU; the 1120 ms variant adds delay and gains nothing.

=== Gates for a streaming model

Gates set in the plan before the measurements. RTF, latency and memory are taken from `podlodka`; the accuracy gate is the plan's "clean WER" and uses `fleurs-clean`:

[cols="3,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Model |RTF at most 0.5 |First text p95 at most 1.5 s |RSS at most 2000 MB |Clean WER within 3 points of best streaming
|T-One |yes (0.04) |yes (1.31 s) |yes (504 MB) |yes (13.7%)
|Nemotron 560 ms |yes (0.13) |*no* (1.59 s) |yes (946 MB) |yes (13.5%, best of all streaming)
|Nemotron 160 ms |yes (0.39) |yes (1.20 s) |yes (946 MB) |yes (14.3%)
|Nemotron 1120 ms |yes (0.08) |*no* (2.05 s) |yes (946 MB) |yes (14.5%)
|===

Only T-One and Nemotron 160 ms pass every gate. T-One does it with a tenth of
the CPU (RTF 0.04 against 0.39), half the memory, and it is 3.8 points more
accurate than Nemotron 160 ms on `podlodka` (14.0% against 17.8%); it is also
3.1–4.1 points ahead of every Nemotron variant on that set. The gates compare
streaming models with each other, so they do not say that streaming models are
accurate enough: they are not as accurate as GigaAM behind a VAD.

=== Decision

*1. Primary engine: GigaAM v3 RNN-T behind Silero VAD.* One shared recogniser
for the whole call, one small VAD instance per audio track, and one worker
(or a small pool) that decodes closed phrases from a common queue; see
"Many participants" below. VAD settings used in the spike: threshold 0.5, minimum silence
0.4 s, minimum speech 0.25 s, maximum phrase 20 s (forces a split so that
phrases stay under GigaAM's 25 s limit). The CTC variant is within 0.4–1.3
points and is slightly better in noise; RNN-T is the default.

*2. Live text layer only if the product needs it.* If the assistant must show
words while a person is speaking, run T-One on the same track as a draft and
replace the draft with GigaAM's text when the phrase closes (two-pass, as in
the sherpa-onnx example `two-pass-speech-recognition-from-microphone.py`).
Costs about 500 MB more memory and one more model to maintain. This layer was
not built or measured in the spike. If the assistant only reacts to finished
phrases, skip it.

*3. Optional engine: Whisper large-v3-turbo.* Slower by a factor of ten and
four times larger, but the best on clean read speech and in noise and the only
candidate here that covers 99 languages. Suitable for post-session
re-transcription or as the engine for non-Russian sessions. Needs language
detection and a chunking strategy for audio longer than 30 s.

*4. Not recommended as the primary engine:*

* Nemotron 3.5 Streaming: less accurate than T-One on spontaneous Russian,
  almost twice the memory and 6–7 busy cores; only the 160 ms variant passes
  every gate, and it needs RTF 0.37–0.39. It remains the candidate for multilingual live
  text, which this spike did not test.
* Parakeet TDT v3: not streaming, more memory than GigaAM, worse in noise.
  Its advantage is language coverage (25 European languages), which matters
  only if non-Russian sessions are in scope.

=== Many participants

The recommendation does not mean one model per participant. The model weights
(about 340 MB for GigaAM) are loaded once. A sherpa-onnx offline recogniser
creates a cheap stream object for every phrase, so all tracks share the same
recogniser. Per participant only the VAD state and a short audio buffer exist.

Why a VAD pipeline scales better than a streaming model:

* An offline model runs only on phrases. Silence costs only the VAD. Cost
  grows with the amount of speech in the call, and in a conversation only one or
  two people speak at a time.
* A streaming model decodes every active stream continuously, silence included,
  and that cost multiplies by the number of participants. T-One at RTF 0.04
  would take about 0.4 of a core-second per second of call for 10 tracks;
  Nemotron 560 ms at RTF 0.13 would reach 1.3, which is slower than real time.

Rough estimate for GigaAM (not measured): RTF 0.03 at about 4.6 busy cores is
about 0.14 core-seconds per second of speech, so ten people speaking at once
would need about 1.4 cores for a moment, and typical load is a fraction of that.

Not measured in the spike: ten simultaneous tracks, the cost of ten VAD
instances (the Silero model file is 0.6 MB, so memory is expected to be small),
and whether one recogniser may be called from several threads (the safe design
is a single decode worker with a queue, which also keeps the order of phrases).
Open the load test as a follow-up before the participant limit is promised.

Alternative: each participant transcribes only their own microphone on their own
machine and sends text. It scales to any group size but needs the model on every
client, so it is not suitable for weak or shared devices and was not evaluated.

=== Integration into the C++/Qt application

*Prebuilt packages do not work with the C++ wrapper.* The latest release is
v1.13.8 (2026-09-10). Its Linux archives named `*-lib` contain only the
libraries; the full archives (for example `shared-no-tts`) also carry the
headers, which are identical to the ones in the source tree. In both, the
libraries are built with the pre-C++11 `std::string` ABI (symbols such as
`sherpa_onnx::cxx::ReadWave(std::string const&)` mangle as `...RKSs`), while a
current GCC emits `std::__cxx11::basic_string`. The C++ wrapper `cxx-api.h`
therefore fails to link (`undefined reference to ... ReadWave`) and, where it
does link, would hand the library `std::string` objects with a different
layout. Qt applications built with a modern toolchain cannot use it this way.

*Two ways that work, both verified on a 106 s Podlodka recording
(GigaAM v3 RNN-T + Silero VAD, 4 threads):*

[cols="3,2,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Integration |API |Phrases |WER % |RTF
|Python, `sherpa-onnx` 1.13.8 wheel |Python |10 |6.7 |0.03
|C++ against the prebuilt shared libraries |plain C API (`c-api.h`) |10 |6.7 |0.028
|C++ with sherpa-onnx built from source through CMake `FetchContent` |C++ API (`cxx-api.h`) |10 |6.7 |0.028
|===

All three produce the same text. The recommended path for Sessio is the third
one: add `sherpa-onnx` with `FetchContent` (tag `v1.13.8`, shared libraries;
Python, tests, binaries, TTS, diarization, WebSocket, PortAudio and GPU
switched off), which builds with the application's own toolchain and ABI and
gives the type-safe C++ wrapper. The spike configured and built it in a few
minutes with 12 parallel jobs; the resulting `lib/` directory is 84 MB. The
plain C API against the prebuilt libraries is the fallback if build time or
dependencies matter more than convenience. Ready code for both is in
`spikes/asr-model-bench/cpp/` (`asr_smoke.cc`, `from-source/asr_smoke_cxx.cc`).

*Model files and settings.* GigaAM v3 RNN-T is the `sherpa-onnx` package
`sherpa-onnx-nemo-transducer-giga-am-v3-russian-2025-12-16` with
`encoder.int8.onnx`, `decoder.onnx`, `joiner.onnx` and `tokens.txt`, loaded as
an offline transducer with `model_type = "nemo_transducer"`. The Silero VAD
model is `silero_vad.onnx` from the same release page. Audio must be 16 kHz
mono; the VAD takes 512-sample windows.

Not verified: building and packaging on Windows and macOS, the effect on the
RPM package size (issue #70) and whether `sherpa-onnx` is available through
vcpkg.

=== Not covered

* Real conversation between two people over a video call: microphone, Opus
  compression, echo and network loss all change the audio. Podlodka is a
  studio-quality podcast.
* Psychotherapy vocabulary and emotional speech.
* The two-pass layer (T-One draft plus GigaAM final) and the speaker-separation
  setup with two LiveKit tracks.
* Load with many participants (for example ten tracks in one call).
* CPU contention with a running video call; every measurement ran alone.
* Windows and macOS; GPU acceleration; other quantisations.
* Small sample: `podlodka` has 63 utterances and `podlodka-long` has 10
  recordings, so differences of 1–2 points between close models are within
  noise. FLEURS may overlap the training data of some models, so weigh the
  Podlodka tables higher.
* Licences were taken from model cards and memory; the OpenMDW licence of
  Nemotron and the Apache-2.0 licence of T-One must be reviewed before shipping.

=== Consequences and next steps

* Open a product issue for a `TranscriptionService` in the call UI (#80):
  per-track VAD, worker thread, phrase queue, a model download and storage
  flow (the models are 170–500 MB and are not bundled with the application).
* Record 20–30 minutes of conversation with consenting colleagues, over a real
  LiveKit call, and rerun the comparison on it before the engine is frozen.
* Decide with the product owner whether word-by-word live text is required;
  that decides whether the T-One layer is built.
* Keep Whisper turbo as the second engine behind the same interface for
  multilingual and post-session use.
